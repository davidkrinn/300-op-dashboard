# Project Brief

## Project Name

Restaurant Franchise Executive Dashboard

## Project Type

Hypothetical interactive dashboard prototype

## One-Sentence Concept

A modern executive dashboard that helps a multi-state Taco Bell and Pizza Hut franchise ownership group (Taco Hut Enterprises) quickly understand sales performance, cost risks, store health, and regional opportunities without manually digging through spreadsheets.

## Core Problem

Franchise leadership teams often need to review performance across many stores, brands, regions, and operational metrics. The data may exist across POS systems, corporate feeds, delivery platforms, labor systems, weather data, event calendars, and financial systems, but the information is fragmented.

The dashboard should simplify that complexity by turning distributed data into a clear, actionable daily snapshot.

## Business Goals

The dashboard should help the ownership group:

1. Manage costs more proactively
2. Understand whether sales are up or down
3. Identify stores at risk
4. Spot regional opportunities
5. Reduce time spent analyzing spreadsheets
6. Help leaders act before small issues become larger problems

## Primary Users

The dashboard is designed for an executive audience made up of:

- Franchise group owners
- Regional managers
- Operations leaders
- Finance leaders
- Legal leaders
- Real estate leaders

All users should initially see the same executive dashboard. Future experiences may include role-specific drilldowns.

## Primary User Need

Users need a fast, clear, interactive view of business performance across:

- States
- Regions
- Brands
- Individual stores
- Sales channels
- Time periods
- Risk categories

The dashboard should answer:

- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- Where should we focus?
- Which stores need attention?
- Which regions are creating opportunities?
- What should we do next?

## Geographic Scope

The dashboard covers Taco Bell and Pizza Hut locations across:

- Minnesota
- Wisconsin
- Iowa
- South Dakota
- North Dakota

## Store Portfolio

| State | Taco Bell Stores | Pizza Hut Stores | Total Stores |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Minnesota | 100 | 90 | 190 |
| Wisconsin | 85 | 70 | 155 |
| Iowa | 87 | 69 | 156 |
| South Dakota | 60 | 50 | 110 |
| North Dakota | 55 | 58 | 113 |
| **Total** | **387** | **337** | **724** |

## Comparison Requirements

The dashboard must allow comparisons across:

- Store vs. store
- Store vs. region
- Taco Bell vs. Pizza Hut
- State vs. state
- Year-over-year

## Most Important KPIs

### Sales

- Gross Sales
- Average Ticket
- Same Store Sales Growth

### Orders

- Total Orders
- Orders Per Hour
- Orders Per Day

### Operations

- Drive-thru Speed
- Order Accuracy
- Labor %
- Food Cost %

### Customer Experience

- Complaint Volume
- App Ratings

### Finance

- EBITDA
- Revenue per Location

## User Experience Principles

The dashboard should be:

- Insight-led, not chart-led
- Fast to scan
- Easy to filter
- Modern and colorful
- Executive-ready
- Highly interactive
- Clear enough for non-technical users
- Useful for both daily review and deeper analysis

## Primary Experience Strategy

The dashboard should open with AI-generated insights before charts.

The first thing users see should be a prioritized summary of what matters today.

Example insight types:

- Performance insight
- Risk insight
- Opportunity insight
- Forecast insight

## Example AI Insights

### Performance Insight

"Minneapolis Taco Bell locations are up 12% due to a Twins home series."

### Risk Insight

"Labor costs exceeded target in 8 stores this week."

### Opportunity Insight

"Drive-thru sales historically increase 18% during severe rain events."

### Forecast Insight

"Projected sales for next weekend are 9% higher due to the State Fair."

## Prototype Scope

The prototype should represent a full product concept, including:

1. Executive homepage
2. Regional view
3. Store-level view
4. Insights center
5. Forecasting
6. Alerts
7. Mobile-friendly experience
8. Regional Manager deep-dive example

## Out of Scope for Initial Prototype

The initial prototype does not need to include:

- Real production data integrations
- Authentication
- Role-based permissions
- Live POS connection
- Real AI model integration
- Back-end infrastructure
- Production-grade forecasting

These may be represented with mock data and simulated functionality.

## Definition of Success

The prototype is successful if it demonstrates how leaders can quickly:

- See what matters today
- Understand sales and cost movement
- identify risk
- compare stores and regions
- spot trends and opportunities
- move from analysis to action faster
